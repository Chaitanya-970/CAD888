How it works

Three signals, one smarter route

Safeway combines real-time data, community reports, and AI-powered explanations to give you a walking route that actually accounts for safety.

01 — SCORE

Time-sensitive safety scoring

Every route segment gets a 0–100 safety score based on lighting, foot traffic, and incident history. The score changes by the hour — the same street at noon and midnight are not the same street.

02 — REPORT

Anonymous community signals

Log a felt-unsafe moment in seconds — no account needed, no police report required. Reports are geotagged, time-stamped, and privacy-softened so they help without stigmatizing.

03 — EXPLAIN

AI-powered route explanations

An LLM agent reads the safety data and gives you a plain-language explanation of why one route scored higher — verified against the underlying numbers so it never hallucinates.

About us

Built in Bhubaneswar, for Bhubaneswar

We're a three-person team who believe pedestrian safety shouldn't depend on word of mouth. Safeway is our answer — a system that turns informal local knowledge into real routing intelligence.

OUR MISSION

Score routes, not neighborhoods

A single dark stretch shouldn't brand an entire area as unsafe. Safeway attaches safety signals to specific street segments at specific hours — never to zip codes, never to communities. The data stays private: shown only to the person who asked, never published as a stigmatizing overlay.

THE PROBLEM

Maps are blind after dark

Every routing engine optimizes for distance and time. None of them know how a street actually feels to walk at 10 PM — whether there's lighting, foot traffic, or open shops. That judgment gets made informally, passed through group chats and local knowledge. We're making it systematic.

THE TEAM

Three roles, one pipeline

Our hackathon team split the problem into frontend experience, backend infrastructure, and AI integration — each piece feeding the next. The result is a full-stack system where crowdsourced safety signals flow from anonymous reports through geospatial indexing, AI-powered analysis, and time-aware scoring into a map interface that actually helps you decide which way to walk.
